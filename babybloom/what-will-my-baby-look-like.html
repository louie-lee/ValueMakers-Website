What Will My Baby Look Like? 2 Ways to See Your Baby's Face Before Birth

For expecting parents and curious couples · Updated July 2026

"What will my baby look like?" It's the question every parent-to-be asks — lying awake at night, holding a sonogram to the light, studying your partner's smile and wondering whose nose the baby will get. Until recently the only answer was wait and see. Now AI gives you two genuinely good ways to preview your baby's face before the big day.

Way 1: From your 3D ultrasound (most accurate)

If you're already pregnant and have a 3D or 4D ultrasound, this is the closest you can get to your baby's real face. The scan already contains your baby's actual facial structure — AI simply renders it as a lifelike photo, adding the skin, light and detail the scan can't capture.

In BabyBloom's Ultrasound Mode, you upload the scan photo, pick your preferences, and the AI reads the real geometry of your baby's face — nose shape, lip curve, eye depth — to produce a portrait-quality 2K image. Because it works from your baby's features, the result is personal, not generic.

Full walkthrough: How to turn your ultrasound into a baby picture.

Way 2: From Mom and Dad's photos (no ultrasound needed)

Not pregnant yet, or your scan is weeks away? Parent Photo Mode answers the question from the other direction: upload one clear photo of each parent, and the AI blends your features into a predicted baby face.

BabyBloom also shows a resemblance breakdown — for example, Mom 54% / Dad 46% — so you can finally settle the "they'll have your eyes" debate with numbers. It's the modern version of the game every couple plays, and the result is genuinely shareable.

What actually determines what your baby looks like?

Genetics, of course — but not as a simple 50/50 blend. Features like eye color, hair color, dimples and face shape each follow their own inheritance patterns, which is why siblings can look so different. That's also why an AI preview is best treated as a realistic possibility, not a promise:

  • Ultrasound-based predictions are grounded in your baby's real facial structure — the strongest signal available before birth.
  • Parent-photo predictions are a plausible blend of your features — perfect for fun, announcements, and the "whose nose?" debate.
